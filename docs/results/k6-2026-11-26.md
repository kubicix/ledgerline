# k6 results, 2026-11-26

The seven scenarios of [testing.md section 5](../testing.md#5-k6-scenarios), run against the Compose stack on one
desktop PC that also runs the load generator. Targets were fixed before any measurement and are not changed here. A
missed target is published as a miss, with the reason as far as it is known.

**These are not capacity numbers.** k6 and the twelve long-running containers of the stack (the telemetry container included) share one CPU and
one consumer disk. The numbers show how this system behaves on this machine and where it breaks first. They say
nothing about what the design can do on production hardware.

Each run used a named Compose project (`docker compose -p <name> --profile app up -d --build --wait`) and fresh
idempotency keys. topup-steady, transfer-steady, broker-outage and the spike each had a fresh project;
transfer-hot-account and payment-mix ran one after the other on the same project.

### How the numbers are read

- **Accept latency** is `http_req_duration{phase:load,op:accept}`: the POST that starts a saga, until its 202.
- **Saga completion** is `saga_completion_ms`: from the 202 until `GET /api/payments/{id}` returns a final status,
  polled every 100 ms. A saga that is not final after 30 s (120 s for the spike probes, 150 s for the broker-outage probes) is recorded with the time
  waited, so a p95 equal to the timeout means "most sagas did not finish within the timeout", not a measured p95.
  Where that happens, the server-side time from `created_at` to `updated_at` of the payment row is given beside it.
- **Throughput** has no k6 threshold, because k6's `rate` divides by the whole run including setup and teardown.
  It is computed as started iterations divided by the load phase (each iteration is one accepted POST), with
  `dropped_iterations` showing how much of the offered rate k6 could not start because every VU was busy. Completed
  sagas inside the load window come from the Payments database. In the scenarios that poll every saga, the started
  rate is a ceiling set by k6, not a property of the system: when every saga waits the full 30 s poll timeout, k6
  can start at most maxVUs / 30 s, which is 10 per second with 300 VUs (topup-steady) and 13.3 with 400 VUs. The
  throughput verdict therefore rests on completed sagas per second.
- **Outbox lag** (`ledgerline.outbox.lag`) is read from the service databases as `processed_at - occurred_at` of
  every outbox row written during the load phase. This is the same interval the metric records. Prometheus could not
  be used: the OpenTelemetry collector inside `otel-lgtm` was killed for memory early in the load phase of the topup-steady, transfer-steady and broker-outage runs (see
  [Telemetry under load](#telemetry-under-load)).
- **ManualReview** is counted by k6 and confirmed from `payments_db` after the backlog drained.
- **Trial balance** is checked by every scenario's teardown through `GET /api/ledger/trial-balance`, and again from
  `accounts_db` (`sum(balance_minor)` per currency).

Raw k6 summary exports are in [raw/](raw/).

## Why the targets were missed

Correctness held in every run: 0 failed requests (the broker outage included), 0 payments in ManualReview, a
trial balance of 0 in every currency, every outbox row relayed, no payment with a failure reason, and every accepted payment reached a final
state (the mix's uncaptured authorizations stay Authorized until their TTL). The misses are all latency and throughput, and three causes explain them.

1. **The disk stalls under fsync load.** Every saga step waits on an fsync: a Postgres commit, and a RabbitMQ quorum
   queue publisher confirm. Under 400 to 550 flushes per second the DRAM-less NVMe behind the Docker Desktop VM
   slows down for about 17 s every 55 to 90 s; a single commit then takes 60 to 100 ms instead of 1.4 ms. This was
   reproduced with a synthetic fsync load without Ledgerline running. Moving the telemetry stores to memory
   (commit `e06fedc`) cut the stall time by about a third, but the remaining flushes are the durability guarantees
   themselves.
2. **The outbox relay publishes one message at a time.** `OutboxRelay` and `RabbitMqOutboxPublisher` publish a row,
   wait for the broker's confirm, then mark the row processed, row by row. One relay instance therefore moves at
   most one message per confirm round trip, and the round trip is an fsync on the broker. Payments writes the most
   outbox rows per saga (commands to Risk, the bank step, Accounts, and the payment events), so its relay is the
   first to fall behind. In topup-steady its outbox lag p95 was 61 s while Accounts was 0.37 s and Risk 0.08 s. This
   is a design limit of the simple relay, not a bug; batching confirms is the known fix and is out of scope here.
3. **The load generator competes with the system.** The spec polls every saga every 100 ms. At 50 to 100 sagas per
   second with sagas taking tens of seconds, k6 ran 300 to 400 VUs and sent about 1 300 to 2 000 requests per second,
   almost all polls, through the same gateway and Payments instance, on the same CPU. Once every VU was busy polling,
   k6 dropped the rest of the offered rate, so in the polled scenarios the system was sent only 10 to 13.5 sagas
   per second, not the 50 to 100 offered.

The telemetry container was one more competitor for CPU: its OpenTelemetry collector grew until it was killed for
memory in the topup-steady, transfer-steady and broker-outage runs. In a retry with a 6 GiB limit it used about 6 to 7 cores (580-720 %) worth of CPU near its limit (see [Telemetry under load](#telemetry-under-load)).

### topup-steady

50 top-ups per second for 5 min, each with the bank authorize and capture step. Project `ledgerline-m3-k6-t10`,
commit `d165fea`, load phase 10:35:49 to 10:40:49 UTC. Raw: [raw/topup-steady-2026-11-26.json](raw/topup-steady-2026-11-26.json).

| Target | Value | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failed requests | < 0.1 % | 0 of 664 964 | pass |
| Accept latency p95 / p99 | < 150 / < 400 ms | 359.44 / 710.47 ms | miss |
| Top-up saga p95 | < 2 000 ms | 30 219.9 ms: the 30 s timeout (2 998 timed out, 34 completed in time); server side p50 176.1 s, p95 196.2 s | miss |
| Outbox lag p95 | < 500 ms | Payments 61 029 ms (10 334 rows), Accounts 370 ms, Risk 82 ms | miss |
| ManualReview | 0 | 0 (k6 and database) | pass |
| Trial balance | 0 | 0 | pass |

- **Load reached:** 3 032 of 15 000 offered iterations started (10.1 per second); 11 969 were dropped because all
  300 VUs were polling unfinished sagas from 11 s into the load.
- **Completed:** 1 129 top-ups inside the load window (3.8 per second), the other 1 903 in the 132 s after it; all
  3 032 completed.
- **Explanation:** the Payments relay (cause 2) is the bottleneck. A top-up writes about 3.4 outbox rows in Payments,
  and each waits for a broker confirm that is an fsync (cause 1). Payments' lag grew to a minute while Accounts and
  Risk stayed below half a second. The accept latency includes the Payments commit, which slows down in the stall
  windows, and shares the CPU with 1 300 polls per second (cause 3).

Top-ups and transfers, alternating, offered at 50 per second for 3 min as accept-only POSTs, plus one probe saga per
second polled to completion (150 s timeout). `scripts/broker-outage.sh` stopped the project's RabbitMQ 75 s into the

Raw: [raw/broker-outage-2026-11-26.json](raw/broker-outage-2026-11-26.json).

| Target | Value | Measured | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failed requests | < 0.1 % | 0 of 159 469 | pass |
| Accept latency p95 / p99 | < 150 / < 400 ms | 119.7 / 224.52 ms | pass |
| Acceptance continues during the outage | all POSTs accepted | 1 500 of 1 500 POSTs inside the window answered 202 | pass |
| Recovery after the outage (backlog drained) | < 60 s | k6: at least 148.1 s (probe p50 94.1 s, 58 probes); database: the last payment of the run completed at 11:14:21.6, 694 s after the window closed | miss |
| Top-up / transfer saga p95 (probes) | < 2 000 / < 1 000 ms | 150.1 s: the probe timeout (97 timed out, 17 completed) | miss |
| ManualReview | 0 | 0 (k6 and database) | pass |
| Trial balance | 0 | 0 | pass |

- **Acceptance held.** All 9 000 offered POSTs were sent and every one was accepted (the 66 dropped iterations
  were probes: 114 of 180 ran), including the
  1 500 sent while the broker was down: a POST commits the payment and its outbox row locally and does not need the
  broker. The gateway kept routing to Payments through the outage.
- **Recovery missed, and on this machine it cannot be separated from overload.** Completions stopped while the broker
  was down (0 in the 11:02:30 bucket, 8 in the next) and resumed at 631 in the first 30 s after the broker was
  healthy. But 50 per second is already more than this machine completes in steady state (topup-steady and
  transfer-steady completed 4 to 6 per second), so a backlog was building before the outage and kept building after
  it. The k6 recovery number is a lower bound: a probe that timed out is recorded with its timeout, and the slowest
  probes accepted before the window closed had still not finished 148 s after it. The database view: all 9 104 payments created in the load phase completed, the last at 11:14:21.6 UTC, about 10 min after the load ended; server side p95 was 627.0 s for top-ups and 364.3 s for transfers, and the Payments outbox lag p95 was 164 937 ms (14 634 rows).
- **Nothing was lost.** Every payment accepted during the run reached a final state after the drain, with no
  ManualReview and a trial balance of 0. The system test `FailureSystemTests.Broker_outage_loses_no_money` proves the
  same property without load; this run adds the load.

## Telemetry under load

**The OpenTelemetry collector in `otel-lgtm` runs out of memory under load.** This was found during these runs and
is not fixed.

- **At the 4 GiB limit.** In all three projects run today (topup-steady, transfer-steady and broker-outage),
  `docker inspect` of `otel-lgtm` reported `OOMKilled=true`.
  - In the topup-steady project the killed process was the collector. It was missing from the container while
    Grafana, Prometheus, Tempo and Loki kept running, and the last metric sample arrived about 40 s into the load.
  - No new metrics or traces arrived after that. This is why the dashboard screenshot holds only the first 40 s or so
    of the load, and why outbox lag was measured from the databases.
- **Retried at 6 GiB.** Raising the limit to 6 GiB was tried on one more transfer-steady run (project
  `ledgerline-m3-k6-t10d`; its numbers are not published). It still ran out of memory.
  - The container went from 1.2 GiB at load start to the 6 GiB limit within about 1.5 min, about 4.6 GiB per minute.
  - It then sat at the limit for about 3 min, using 580 to 720 % CPU, before the kernel killed the collector. After
    the kill the collector was gone again.
  - The telemetry stores were not the cause. The capped in-memory stores held about 0.28 GiB in total. The growth
    was the collector's own memory.
  - The limit was set back to 4 GiB, because a higher limit only delays the kill.
- **The likely fix is on the collector side**, for example a `memory_limiter` processor, or trace sampling or
  batching limits in the collector configuration. It is left to the owner.
- **Effect on the published numbers.** While the collector was alive and growing, it competed with the system under
  test for CPU, the same way k6 does. Numbers from the first minutes of each load phase are therefore pessimistic.
  After the kill that competitor was gone, so this effect is not constant over a run.
- **Older runs.** Whether the same OOM happened in the earlier runs (transfer-hot-account, payment-mix and spike) is
  unknown: those runs did not check `otel-lgtm`.
